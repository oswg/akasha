Once upon a time, at the foot of a great mountain range, there sat a small village that was on the whole full of peace and harmony. But there was in this village a division between those who were well-to-do and those who were not so well-to-do. Among those who were well-to-do, there was a family that had a young daughter about ten years old, and her name was Christina. She was well-looked after in the sense that she had all that she needed to flourish and grow. She had good clothes to wear. She had a tutor to provide her with a good education. She was well-fed and taught the niceties of existing as a member of her own class. And she lived within the confines of a large estate completely surrounded by a fence through which, however, she could peer to see the goings-on of the outside world. It happened also that on the other side of this fence there was a young boy just her age by the name of James. He, however, was born in difficult circumstances. He did not always have food on the table. His clothes were ragged, and his speech was somewhat coarse from the standpoint of those who are sophisticated in such matters. But he had an open heart, and he had a yearning that was brought into a high state of passion as he gazed through this fence and saw this beautiful young girl, his age, peering back at him from her side of the fence. To him, she seemed the picture of everything he would wish for, accomplishment and privilege to meet his every desire. But from the standpoint of Christina, James had something that she could only long for, for he had a kind of freedom that to her seemed more desirable than the totality of everything she was born to. In this mountain village, from long periods of time preceding the lives of Christina and James, there had been established traditions of festivals in this village where, though under normal circumstances, those of means and those without tended to keep to themselves, they would come together. And this festival was something that did not happen very often, but every few years or so. And though these two children had been present in the past at a very young age, a sense of remembrance started to come into their awareness as preparations began to unfold around them. And a certain sense of excitement in seeing things change and seeing things come together in a way they normally did not. In the festival, the day of the festival, Christina found herself going into a part of the town that she was not accustomed to go into, and found that people were gathering together. Here again she caught sight of the boy that she, of whom did not know the name, and there they met eyes and had a mutual recognition of each other, and a curiosity and desire to interact arose. The lack of a fence between these two children created within each of them a recognition of their new opportunities. Opportunities to be different people than their village had ordained for them. And their curiosity for the other dislodged and dispensed with those hesitations handed down to them by propriety and elders. And so they walked towards one another and asked each other's names, and as they familiarized themselves with each other, this began to create a stir amongst the festival goers who were unaccustomed to the mixing of these two populations, for they were quite happy with the fences in their lives, and to see someone so young so easily tread past the fence caused them to doubt their own beliefs and identities, and so they began to question the young man and the young girl, throwing accusations at them that were ridiculous, but designed to justify their fears that they felt within, and when this intimidation did not stop the curiosity and friendliness of the two children, they decided to take action. We take up this story at a point of tension and fear for the adults of the village who had decided that these children had violated the conventions so deeply, while Christina and James were somewhat oblivious. Christina had been raised with conventions designed to reinforce her worldview, her lifestyle, and had never been guided to consider the point of view of someone else who may not be in her comfortable estate. She found a stark connection with James, who didn't care who made her dress, where she lived. He was simply interested in her name, and what game she may like to play. She had never considered that someone outside her world would have such simple curiosity and joy without the comfort of nice clothes and a full belly. So as the adults positioned themselves to intervene, James grabbed Christina by the hand to ask her if she would like to explore the forest nearby. As the two ran off, some of the fervor of the adults seemed to fade away. The futility of their intervention of the simple joy of children seemed a bit silly. And while there was no great coming together of the adults, it was decided that they would allow the children to explore their paths.
